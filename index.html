Is resisting distraction
more effortful?

A colour-wheel working-memory task paired with an effort-discounting choice task showed that people find shutting out distraction more costly than flexibly updating what they hold in mind.

Method Effort-discounting paradigm, comparison of discounting-curve models of subjective effort cost, and mixed-effects models.

Working memoryCognitive effort

Pharmacological interventionsTwo double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover studies

Understanding drug effects
on brain and behaviour

Propranolol increased motivation for cognitive stability in Parkinson’s disease, and pupil size during the task predicted later willingness to exert effort. In 100 healthy adults, methylphenidate and reward both sped up responses, but methylphenidate reduced impulsive responses while reward increased them. Methylphenidate left behavioural and neural sensitivity to reward unchanged and instead boosted anticipatory parietal activity, pointing to enhanced attention.

Method Frequentist and Bayesian mixed-effects models; fMRI with permutation-based inference.
